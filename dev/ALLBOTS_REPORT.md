# Mech Royale bot review: evidence-ranked changes for giddy

**Scope.** Read-only review of the provided `allbots/` snapshot and `gobase/brain619.go`. No repository bot, simulator, or test code was run. Recommendations are ordered by expected impact on the 16-bot FINAL first, then by official practice impact. “Live FFA” means recorded/showdown behavior; simulator league results are labeled separately and are not treated as final placement evidence.

## Executive read

The reports support a clear split in objectives. In live FFA, the higher floor comes from quiet movement, hard cover, breaking contact long enough to heal, and taking only selected fights. A second weapon—especially a laser—improves the loadout ceiling, and kills can snowball through dropped cubes and specials. Practice is a kill race against a cooperative squad, so forcing FFA survival behavior into practice is a known regression.

`brain619.go` already has much of the strong baseline: 4.6 m/s quiet walking while moving home, hard-cover retreat, laser-charge-aware positioning, laser-first selection, practice laser seeking, projectile/laser dodge, lead aiming, and separate practice parameters. The most useful new work is therefore narrow and mode-gated: use sound and a wider threat radius when rotating in FFA, add a safe early FFA laser detour, wait longer before re-engaging after retreat, and move nanites ahead of weapon upgrades only in FFA.

No reviewed repository presents a controlled, official FINAL placement A/B for these changes. The live evidence is observational, and simulator league scores below are only secondary clues.

## FINAL recommendations, ranked by expected placement impact

### 1. Add sound-aware threat avoidance to noncombat FFA movement

**Expected impact: high on early survival/floor; confidence: medium-low; risk: medium.**

**Evidence.** The consolidated giddy-fork `KNOWLEDGE.md` summarizes 15 showdowns/240 mech-matches: quiet movement and lower speed correlate with better points (quiet +0.37, speed −0.48), and proximity to the zone center also correlates with points (−0.46 for distance). Its more detailed early-showdown file records 13/16 mechs creeping at 4.6 m/s, just below the 5 m/s step-sound threshold, and observes that boost can expose a mech from farther away. These are correlations, not causal trials; the early file itself warns that its two-match sample is small (`allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/KNOWLEDGE.md:3-10,29-35`; `allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/knowledge/topteams.md:11-12,25-29`).

A useful implementation clue comes from Xero’s local FFA league simulator, not from a real FINAL: repelling from known non-engaging enemies plus recent sound bearings improved its mixed league mean from about 11.82 to 12.01 at a 200 m radius; the 260 m radius scored 12.22, but the response was not monotonic. The same report describes 82% multi-attacker deaths in its practice-like squad model. Treat this as a hypothesis for range selection, not proof of FFA gain (`allbots/arnabxero_xero-mech-royale/STATUS.md:44-77`).

**Current giddy behavior.** `step` already slows movement to 4.6 m/s when quietly moving home (`gobase/brain619.go:831-839`). `homeCost` penalizes remembered mechs only inside 80 m, with tracks retained for up to 20 s, and it scores hard-cover exposure (`gobase/brain619.go:1802-1815`). `Obs.Sounds` is parsed in `types.go`, but `observe` never processes it (`gobase/types.go:225-230,279-294`; `gobase/brain619.go:232-321`).

**Change.** In `observe`, retain short-lived sound contacts using `Kind`, `Bearing`, `Dist`, and `Age`; treat each as a broad bearing sector rather than an exact point because the distance is banded and the measured bearing noise is about 9.5° (n=19). In FFA only, add a smooth avoidance penalty to `homeCost` and safe loot destination scores for known non-engaging enemies and recent enemy gun/laser/boost sounds. Start with 150–200 m for a visible non-engager and decay sound risk over roughly 3 s. Keep zone urgency, an active winning fight, and immediate escape higher priority than avoidance. Do not boost merely to get away from a sound.

**Risk.** Overreacting to a coarse sound band can send the bot toward the zone edge, into a worse group, or away from a valuable kill. Use the cue to bias route/hold selection, not to chase or flee blindly. The 40/90/320/380/420/600 m sound ranges (steps/boost-or-jet/gun/laser/mortar/death-or-drop) and near/mid/far bands are documented at `allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/KNOWLEDGE.md:93-98`.

### 2. Seek a laser early in FFA, with a strict abort condition

**Expected impact: high if the bot otherwise remains gun-only; confidence: medium; risk: medium-high.**

**Evidence.** The 240-mech showdown summary counts 103 laser kills and reports mean points of 9.18 for gun+laser versus 4.38 for gun-only (n=143 and 64; survivorship bias applies). The earlier two-match record similarly notes that each gun-only early death it inspected had not found a second weapon, while explicitly warning that the loadout comparison is biased by survival (`allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/KNOWLEDGE.md:26-28`; `allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/knowledge/topteams.md:30-33`). In official practice, Juan’s laser-first change raised one submission from 4.96 to 5.10; it was neutral in the alternate “seek” profile. PNP’s selected 4.48 entry included laser pickup priority, longer laser engagement, release-time aim, and lethal partial release, so it supports the package but does not isolate laser seeking (`allbots/JuanQuenga_buildathon-mech-royale/eval/RESULTS.md:14-22`; `allbots/pnp6113_mech-royale-bot/docs/EXPERIMENTS.md:24-35`).

**Current giddy behavior.** The default FFA profile has `LaserFirst=true`, but `LaserSeek` is zero and `LootRadius` is 90 m (`gobase/brain619.go:157-164`). `chooseLoot` only extends laser pickup range when `LaserSeek > LootRadius`; the practice profile sets `LaserSeek=240` and `LaserUp=true` (`gobase/brain619.go:1710-1725,1952-1969`). Thus giddy already prefers a laser it sees nearby, and already seeks one in practice, but does not have that longer detour in FFA.

**Change.** In the FFA parameter profile, set a temporary early `LaserSeek` around 150 m (test 120–175 m) while the second slot is empty, with a time limit around the first 35–45 s. Permit the detour only when no enemy is engaging/aiming at us, we have not just taken damage, the pickup is reachable inside the next circle, and its approach does not pass through a known threat sector. Abort immediately on a nearby hostile, a laser charge aimed at us, or urgent zone travel. Retain the existing laser-first pickup ranking.

**Risk.** Weapon runs can expose the bot and spend energy/time while opponents converge. Do not copy the 220–400 m seek ranges from other bots as a default: official practice experiments disagree on range, and a range change is not a clean proxy for final behavior.

### 3. Require a real recovery before re-engaging after an FFA retreat

**Expected impact: high on survival floor; confidence: medium; risk: medium.**

**Evidence.** The live showdown notes show both a fighting winner and a hiding giddy bot breaking off and recovering toward full HP between fights; the recorded examples regain roughly 1,000–1,700 HP over 20 s. The consolidated rules report 45 HP/s after 5 s without dealing or taking damage, and 72 HP/s after 3.5 s with nanites (`allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/knowledge/topteams.md:38-42,53-60`; `allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/KNOWLEDGE.md:34-35`). This is live trajectory evidence, not a controlled A/B.

**Current giddy behavior.** FFA defaults to `RecoverHP=.8`, but `step` also clears retreat after 7 threat-free seconds once HP exceeds 55%, even when the configured recover threshold is higher (`gobase/brain619.go:157-160,634-640`). Giddy already finds hard cover and holds there when retreating (`gobase/brain619.go:703-721,1475-1575`).

**Change.** In the confirmed FFA branch, remove or raise the 55% fallback; resume proactive engagements only after reaching about 85–90% HP, while retaining the existing hard-cover/zone checks. Keep the shorter threshold for practice. Do not use a fixed “stand still to heal” action in the open: the value is the no-hit window that starts regen, with cover protecting against hits.

**Risk.** Excessive recovery can pass winnable third-party opportunities and leave too little time to heal in a short match. Make the threshold conditional on current crowd/threat count and allow finishing a clearly lethal, low-risk target; do not let it override zone escape.

### 4. Put nanites before marginal weapon upgrades in FFA only

**Expected impact: medium; confidence: medium-low; risk: medium.**

**Evidence.** The live notes say winners take plating, armor, and nanites early; recorded live upgrade summaries also favor defensive picks among winners (`allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/knowledge/topteams.md:38-42,55-56`; `allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/KNOWLEDGE.md:87-88,188-189`). Alex’s local FFA simulator reports a +0.425 change over 40 paired games for a health/regen/sustain-first candidate, while an alternate order regressed by 1.25 points over eight pairs. Evans has a similar +0.425 over 40 local FFA games and explicitly says it is surrogate-only. These results are suggestive, not real FINAL placement (`allbots/alexwilldev_mechroyale-bot/PARAMS_EFFECTS.md:67-81`; `allbots/Evans-Kwashie_mech-royale-bot/docs/7-POINT-CANDIDATE.md:8-25,31-37`).

**Current giddy behavior.** The default order puts `gun_damage` before `nanites`; `upgradeOrderNano` already provides the desired `plating, armor, nanites, laser_damage, laser_charge…` order, selected when `NanoUp=true` (`gobase/brain619.go:442-458,466-490`). The practice profile sets `LaserUp=true`, which prioritizes laser upgrades before nanites (`gobase/brain619.go:1952-1977`).

**Change.** Set `NanoUp=true` only in the FFA parameter profile, or add an explicit FFA upgrade order that selects plating, armor, nanites, then weapon throughput. Leave practice ordering untouched.

**Risk.** Less early damage can cost kills and cube snowball. The local FFA results are small and the alternate order’s regression argues for moving nanites just one place rather than trying to prioritize movement/energy upgrades wholesale.

### 5. Add a gated third-party / carrier target bonus; collect the reward only when safe

**Expected impact: medium-high ceiling, low-medium confidence; risk: high.**

**Evidence.** The consolidated live data finds kills, damage, and cubes correlate positively with points; the specific m5 recording shows Xero convert kills into 23 cubes, and the m6 recording shows giddy win with four kills/37 cubes. A kill drops the victim’s cubes plus two and each non-gun weapon, so killing a laser/lightning carrier can both remove a dangerous weapon and grant it (`allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/KNOWLEDGE.md:29-37`; `allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/knowledge/topteams.md:34-42,64-70`). These are selected match outcomes and cannot establish that chasing kills caused the placements.

**Current giddy behavior.** FFA target scoring is mainly distance/HP, recent damage, and closing velocity; its expected-win `odds()` path is disabled by default (`P.Odds=0`) (`gobase/brain619.go:525-599,560-562`). FFA has no carrier bonus. `fightMove` already biases toward visible cubes within 40 m, and normal loot still assigns value to cubes beyond 10, but only weakly (`gobase/brain619.go:1395-1399,1448-1451,1727-1734`). `FightLoot` is zero outside the practice profile (`gobase/brain619.go:1964`).

**Change.** In FFA only, add a modest target-score bonus when an enemy is demonstrably occupied with another mech (aim points away / it is receiving damage) or carries laser/lightning, and only when our HP is banked, a second threat is not in close range, and there is a hard-cover exit. Reuse the existing odds model to gate an otherwise optional target (`step` already has a hook at lines 560–562). After a kill, favor the resulting nearby cube/special pickup only if no immediate shooter has line of sight; do not set a large unconditional `FightLoot` radius. A small FFA-only cube goal of 15 is a testable option because it reaches the next upgrade tier; going much higher trades hitbox size for thresholds.

**Risk.** This can turn a good position into a 2v1. Extra cubes increase size by 5% each without a cap, so more cubes are not free. The live correlations are not causal, and Juan’s cube-chase tests were noisy (one 5.46, repeat 5.31, with a similar policy mean to baseline); do not turn the bonus into a universal hunt order (`allbots/JuanQuenga_buildathon-mech-royale/eval/RESULTS.md:182-189`).

### 6. Consider lethal partial laser release in FFA, using giddy’s existing conservative calculation

**Expected impact: medium-low; confidence: low-medium; risk: low-medium if the kill check remains conservative.**

**Evidence.** PNP’s official 4.48 submission included laser release-time aim and lethal partial release as part of a larger package; the package had no regression in its 32-game modeled FFA check, which is not a live FINAL result (`allbots/pnp6113_mech-royale-bot/docs/EXPERIMENTS.md:35,79-85`). Jishnu’s README claims an official practice jump from 5.73 to 5.81 for early lethal laser releases, but the file also contains 2026-11-22 results, one day after this snapshot’s date, so I do not count that as current-date evidence (`allbots/JishnuMadav_mech-royale/README.md:90-117`).

**Current giddy behavior.** The partial-charge kill branch exists, but is disabled in the default FFA profile; the practice profile turns it on with `Lethal=true` and `LethalK=0.7` (`gobase/brain619.go:1001-1019,1967-1974`). The charge threshold already uses a 0.051 s tolerance against `Stats.Charge`, so the Jishnu “laser rounding fix” should not be copied blindly. Live mechanics specify 0.75 s base charge and 0.56 s with laser-charge upgrade (`allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/KNOWLEDGE.md:75-76`; `gobase/brain619.go:1001-1022`).

**Change.** Trial `Lethal=true` in FFA while preserving the existing predicted-damage discount and aim-tolerance requirement. Keep a fully charged release when a partial shot cannot clear the predicted HP. Do not start or continue a charge as the retreat/zone policy is trying to escape without accounting for the engine’s half-speed charge movement.

**Risk.** Releasing early can fail to kill due to armor/rounding and lose a full-charge opportunity. The source gains are bundled and there is no controlled live FFA comparison.

### 7. Keep supply drops as a conditional, low-priority FFA option

**Expected impact: low; confidence: low; risk: medium-high.**

The live FFA data says drops offer three cubes plus full HP/energy, but an 8 m capture ring held for four seconds is dangerous in open laser sightlines; winners only take them when the ring can be held behind hard cover (`allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/KNOWLEDGE.md:80-88,203-208`). Giddy’s default `UseDrops=false` (`gobase/brain619.go:157-160`). Other teams’ practice deltas are tiny: Null’s 220→300 m drop reach is 4.44→4.46, but extending landed-drop slack regresses to 4.38; DM’s reach steps from 150→200 m are 4.10→4.17 (`allbots/nullat0m7_mech-royale/PATTERNS.md:17-19`; `allbots/dmclaughlin-bit_mech-bot/EXPERIMENTS.md:7-9`). This is not enough to justify open-ring capture in FFA.

If tested, enable it only when no known enemy/sound threat is near the ring, the circle deadline leaves time, and the ring is behind hard cover. Keep the current arrival-time check; do not loosen landed-drop slack.

## Practice recommendations, ranked

### 1. Keep the practice/FFA policy split intact

This is the most important practice safeguard. Null’s official practice record scored 4.46 for its normal practice behavior and 1.75 after applying ghost/survival behavior from the first tick (−2.71); its FFA-only ghost profile was kept separate (`allbots/nullat0m7_mech-royale/PATTERNS.md:9-25`; `allbots/nullat0m7_mech-royale/CHANGELOG.md:10-17`). Giddy already has that split: after detecting practice, `squadParams` disables Ghost and sets laser seeking, laser upgrades, lethal release, farming, and fight-cube behavior (`gobase/brain619.go:323-350,1952-1977`). Keep every FINAL recommendation above behind confirmed non-practice/FFA parameters.

### 2. Preserve giddy’s existing practice laser seek and laser-first upgrade profile

Giddy’s practice profile already sets `LaserSeek=240`, `LaserFirst=true`, `LaserUp=true`, and `Lethal=true` (`gobase/brain619.go:1952-1975`). Juan’s official log measured a 4.96→5.10 increase for laser-first on its greed profile, and PNP’s official top entry includes laser priority/release behavior, though not as isolated tests (`allbots/JuanQuenga_buildathon-mech-royale/eval/RESULTS.md:14,19-22`; `allbots/pnp6113_mech-royale-bot/docs/EXPERIMENTS.md:24-35`). There is no evidence to increase giddy’s already-large 240 m practice seek radius further.

### 3. Test a narrow, practice-only jet-hop route for reachable roof weapons

**Expected impact: low-medium; confidence: medium-low; risk: medium-high.** Juan’s official run that removed jet hops and roof weapons scored 4.88 versus 5.17 for its preceding route configuration (−0.29), although the change bundled route and pickup differences (`allbots/JuanQuenga_buildathon-mech-royale/eval/RESULTS.md:21-26`). Giddy’s loot eligibility rejects pickups more than 3.2 m above `GroundAt`, which excludes many roof items (`gobase/brain619.go:1616-1619`). A practice-only experiment could allow a roof weapon only where an existing reachable jump/jet route can be completed before zone urgency, and prefer laser/second-slot weapons over a crate detour. Do not add combat hops: Juan’s combat-hop submission was −0.25 (`RESULTS.md:25-26`).

This is a practice-only candidate. In the early live FFA sample, roof/air time was under 2% for almost every mech and nobody camped on a roof (`allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/knowledge/topteams.md:43-44`); it should not be part of the FINAL plan without new FFA evidence.

### 4. Try bounded practice supply-drop reach, but leave landed-drop slack alone

Giddy has `UseDrops=false` by default and the practice profile does not enable it (`gobase/brain619.go:157-160,1952-1977`). The practice evidence is small but directionally positive for bounded reach: DM’s 200 m step scored 4.17 versus 4.10 at 150 m, and Null’s 300 m reach scored 4.46 versus 4.44 at 220 m. Null’s 30 s landed-drop slack regressed to 4.38 (`allbots/dmclaughlin-bit_mech-bot/EXPERIMENTS.md:7-9`; `allbots/nullat0m7_mech-royale/PATTERNS.md:17-19`). A cautious practice-only trial should cap distance near 200–260 m, preserve the current arrival-time check, and abort if a fight/zone deadline becomes urgent. Expected gain is small; chasing a late landed drop can cost more than the heal/cubes return.

### 5. Do not slow giddy’s combat movement or lengthen strafe cycles on the strength of one score

Juan’s official log shows combat movement capped at 6 m/s scoring 4.77 versus a roughly 5.4 policy mean, and standing still to fire scoring 4.19. Its 3–5 s strafe-reversal result looked +0.35 in one draw, but three reruns averaged 5.42 versus a base mean of 5.38; 6–10 s reversals scored worse in one draw (`allbots/JuanQuenga_buildathon-mech-royale/eval/RESULTS.md:30-37,170-180`). Xero also reports its faster-strafe practice variant at 3.06 versus 3.75. Giddy already uses 4.6 m/s only during safe home travel and otherwise lets `fightMove` strafe at full movement (`gobase/brain619.go:831-839,1379-1473`). Keep the quiet travel cap; do not impose it during a practice fight.

The official 170 m laser-snipe test scored 4.92 versus a roughly 5.35 baseline, and Alex’s longer-range settings generally scored below its 110 m laser/90 m gun setting. Giddy’s FFA 85 m laser preference is consistent with live winner impact ranges of 65–71 m. Do not import the 155 m range-control result from Shenoy’s local house-squad simulator (`allbots/JuanQuenga_buildathon-mech-royale/eval/RESULTS.md:181,192-200`; `allbots/alexwilldev_mechroyale-bot/PARAMS_EFFECTS.md:50-56,97-105`; `allbots/ShenoyVaradaraya_mech-royale/sim/strategy-evaluation.md:19-44`; `allbots/mh-anwar_buildathon-mechroyale/docs/knowledge/topteams.md:43-46`).

## Approaches with negative or weak evidence

- **Never engage / hide from tick one:** Null’s official practice score fell from 4.46 to 1.75. Keep mode separation.
- **Long-range laser camping:** Juan’s official 170 m laser result was 4.92; Alex’s official range sweeps also favored shorter ranges. Shenoy’s 155 m result is local-simulator-only and conflicts with official practice evidence.
- **Slow or stationary combat:** Juan’s 6 m/s cap scored 4.77; standing fire scored 4.19. Silent movement is for travel/hold, not a combat speed cap.
- **More retreat by itself:** Shenoy’s simulator shows longer survival while average placement fell from 4.40 to 2.20; practice logs also reject several overly conservative modes. Only use the retreat threshold in the FFA profile.
- **Aggressive FFA engagement:** Xero’s proactive solo duel/farming variants hurt its practice scores; its local league results were neutral or negative. The live sample supports opportunistic kills, not universal aggression.
- **Rooftop camping:** Jishnu’s README reports 0.44 for a rooftop-movement submission; the live FFA summary reports little air time. The README includes next-day (2026-11-22) results relative to this report’s 2026-11-21 snapshot, so treat the sequence as a chronology-conflicted claim, not a fresh result.
- **Overconfident score deltas:** Juan’s same-code reruns and several official logs show that small changes can be trajectory variance. Keep one-variable trials and reruns for changes below roughly 0.2–0.3 points.

## Source coverage and limits

I read the mandated `mh-anwar` `docs/knowledge/topteams.md` and `docs/KNOWLEDGE.md` first, then examined the experiment/results material for JuanQuenga, dmclaughlin-bit (including its experiment ledger and watcher summaries), Evans-Kwashie, alexwilldev, LanceHuang245, arnabxero/Xero, nullat0m7, JishnuMadav, ShenoyVaradaraya, Tanish-Dhankhar_hope, PNP, akshayagg18, bsrikumar855, Muallim, Johnsinned, and the onepizzateam harness. I also checked the requested giddy-fork repositories Th3r0n, waiprotein, and Sandeep. Where these have only a README/code fork or no saved result table, I did not infer a performance improvement from implementation features alone.

The large `dmclaughlin-bit` and some other repositories have stale or conflicting score mentions: `INDEX.tsv` is a list of scores mentioned in READMEs, not a receipt-level official ledger. For example, Juan’s index entry says 6.31 while its official results table in the checked log tops out at 5.48; dmclaughlin-bit’s index says 6.28 while `EXPERIMENTS.md` lists 4.17; Evans has 6.69 in the index but its dated campaign identifies 5.23 as the user-reported current result. Use the cited per-submission score tables and receipts when judging evidence (`allbots/INDEX.tsv`; `allbots/JuanQuenga_buildathon-mech-royale/eval/RESULTS.md:6-42`; `allbots/dmclaughlin-bit_mech-bot/EXPERIMENTS.md:5-15`; `allbots/Evans-Kwashie_mech-royale-bot/docs/7-POINT-CAMPAIGN-2026-11-21.md:1-7,48-50`).

The early `topteams.md` file has two specific matches (32 mech-matches) while consolidated `KNOWLEDGE.md` claims 15 showdowns/240 mech-matches. I cite both at their stated sample sizes. Neither gives a randomized, same-seed intervention. The most reliable actionable evidence remains the official practice submissions; they should not be mistaken for FINAL ranking evidence.
